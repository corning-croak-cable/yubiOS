\begin{abstract}
We describe a family of composed techniques used to audit and prioritize improvement work across a corpus of engineering artifacts (software skills, self-documentation files): (1) a \emph{learned latent curve}, which re-expands a single 1-D ordering coordinate $t$ into a fixed-width $D$-dimensional embedding via a Fourier basis with learned frequencies; (2) \emph{curve-guided recursive self-improvement} (curve-guided RSI), which uses sparse cells of the fitted curve as a prioritization lens for gap-mapping and bounded fixpoint-style editing cycles; (3) a self-documentation variant of the same pipeline, retargeted at heterogeneous memory-file corpora via a per-corpus primitive basis and an explicit item-granularity rule; and (4) the \emph{hyperspherical-harmonic-curve} variant (v2, new), which replaces the incumbent's flat 2-D Fourier surface on $[0,1]^2$ with the canonical orthonormal hyperspherical-harmonic basis on the Riemann sphere $S^2$ (generalizable to $S^N$), and adds a learned M\"obius $\phi_\theta \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ reparameterization of the domain. We give the governing equations for each stage, the empirical validation obtained on the yubiOS software-skill corpus ($N = 49$ to $70$), and the full prior-art verification of the new variant (arXiv:2601.20528 on Spectral Bayesian Regression on the Sphere; OpenReview \texttt{g6UqpVislvH} on Generalized Fourier Features for Coordinate-Based Learning on Manifolds).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Many corpus-auditing problems reduce to the same shape: $N$ items live in a high-dimensional feature space, and a practitioner wants (a) a single scalar ordering of the items and (b) a fixed-width representation indexed by that ordering, so that interpolation and sparse-region detection are well defined. Dimensionality reduction (collapsing features to $t$) and dimensionality \emph{expansion} (re-expanding $t$ into an embedding) are usually treated as separate problems. This paper documents a single closed-form round trip between them and its use as the fitting stage of a larger closed-loop audit pipeline.

\paragraph{What is new in v2.}
v1 (August 4, 2026) covered the three composed techniques above on the yubiOS skill corpus ($N = 62$ to $211$). v2 adds a fourth composition: the \emph{hyperspherical-harmonic-curve} variant, which lifts the parameter manifold from the flat $[0,1]^2$ to the $N$-sphere $S^N$ (the Riemann sphere $\widehat{\mathbb{C}} \cong \mathbb{CP}^1$ at $N=2$). The variant swaps the separable Fourier basis for the canonical hyperspherical-harmonic basis $Y^{S^N}_{l,m}$ (eigenfunctions of $\Delta_{S^N}$ with eigenvalues $-l(l+N-1)$) and adds a learned M\"obius $\phi_\theta \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ reparameterization of the domain that exists only on the Riemann sphere. We validated the variant through five RSI cycles (cycle 1--5) on the yubiOS skill corpus; the matched-parameter ablation FIRES at both phases (relative result at Phase 2 since the Phase 2 holdout $R^2$ is negative for both arms), the M\"obius refinement preserves cross-ratio within $3.08 \times 10^{-7}$ (float64 noise consistent with the PSL(2,C) identity), the sparse-cell detector (equal-area $S^2$ partition + chordal $r \approx 0.095$) runs end-to-end with plausible counts --- the sphere/flat arms use different partitions and radii so counts are not directly comparable --- and both prior-art hits depth-verified via PDF-grep returned 0 novelty-keyword matches.

\paragraph{Relationship to v1 and to unrelated prior work.}
v2 supersedes v1's Stage-1 curve fit with the sphere variant; the curve-guided RSI pipeline, the self-doc variant, the v3 holdout $R^2 = +0.4655$ empirical validation, and the curvature-vs-weight-space distinction from v1 are unchanged. As in v1, we note the technique's distinction from an unrelated prior work on weight-space reparameterization for neural network optimization (arXiv:2607.09967) that shares only surface vocabulary (``curve'', ``curvature'') and no derivation.

\section{The Learned Latent Curve}
\label{sec:curve}

For output dimension $j = 1, \dots, D$ (canonically $D = 384$) and 1-D coordinate $t \in [0, 1]$, the model is
\begin{equation}
z_j(t) \;=\; a_{j,0} \;+\; \sum_{m=1}^{k} \Big( a_{j,m}\, \sin(2\pi f_m t) \;+\; b_{j,m}\, \cos(2\pi f_m t) \Big),
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
with $k$ shared learned frequencies $f_1, \dots, f_k$ and per-output coefficients $a_{j,m}, b_{j,m}$. Stacked over $j$, this is a curve $\gamma : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^D$. Writing the design vector
\begin{equation}
\phi(t) = \big[\, 1,\; \sin(2\pi f_1 t),\, \cos(2\pi f_1 t),\, \dots,\, \sin(2\pi f_k t),\, \cos(2\pi f_k t) \,\big] \in \mathbb{R}^{1+2k},
\label{eq:design}
\end{equation}
the model is $\gamma(t) = C\, \phi(t)$ with coefficient matrix $C \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times (1+2k)}$, giving a parameter count of
\begin{equation}
P \;=\; k \;+\; D(1 + 2k).
\label{eq:paramcount}
\end{equation}
At $D = 384$, $k = 8$: $P = 8 + 384 \times 17 = 6{,}536$ parameters.

\subsection{Closed-form coefficient solve}
\label{sec:closed-form}

With the frequencies $f$ held fixed, the linear coefficient solve is
\begin{equation}
C^\star \;=\; \big( \Phi^\top \Phi + \lambda I \big)^{-1} \Phi^\top Z
\label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times (1+2k)}$ stacks the design vector and $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$ stacks the target vectors. This is a sanity floor for the gradient fit; a fit that is worse than this at the same frequencies means the optimizer failed, not the model.

\subsection{Obtaining $t$: PCA, rank-uniformization, or learned projection}

We obtain $t$ via PCA top-1 (the dominant axis in the 9-D binary primitive-coverage space), then map it to $[0, 1]$ via rank-uniformization. The latter discards gap magnitudes in exchange for a well-conditioned design matrix; v3's empirical validation found rank-uniformization + 9-D binary coverage the best of four target pipelines, beating raw content ($R^2 = -0.155$) and sentence-transformer embeddings ($R^2 = -0.005$).

\section{Curve-Guided Recursive Self-Improvement}
\label{sec:cgrsi}

The closed-loop audit pipeline has four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fit the curve} (Stage 1) --- reuse \S\ref{sec:curve}; this is where v2's variant plugs in.
\item \textbf{Detect sparse cells} (Stage 2) --- a uniform $0.05 \times 0.05$ grid in $[0,1]^2$ (or the v2 equal-area $S^2$ partition + chordal $r \approx 0.095$) partitions the parameter space; cells containing $\leq 1$ item are isolated.
\item \textbf{Dispatch gap-mapping focused on sparse cells} (Stage 3) --- \texttt{negative-skill-space} dispatches a 12-axis gap-map per isolated item.
\item \textbf{Apply recursive-self-improvement cycles on the top-priority gaps} (Stage 4) --- capped at 3 cycles per run, fixpoint at \texttt{no new substantive gaps AND old gaps closed AND no new anti-patterns}.
\end{enumerate}

Re-fitting after RSI closes gaps (Stage 5) should move the curve: $\text{sparse\_cell\_count}_{\text{post}} < \text{sparse\_cell\_count}_{\text{pre}}$ is the success metric. v3 cycle-5 measured a pre/post delta of $-9$ sparse cells ($21 \to 12$) on the 63-skill corpus, validating the loop.

\section{Self-Documentation Variant}
\label{sec:self}

A special case of the same pipeline applies to the agent's own memory files (SELF.md, SELF-CHANGELOG.md, USER\_PREFERENCES.md, etc.). The per-corpus primitive basis is derived from the file structure; the granularity rule is that each memory file is one corpus item (with sub-decomposition if $N < 20$). Cadence fires every 5 self-mode shipping turns or weekly Sunday 9 AM Pacific, whichever first.

\section{Hyperspherical-Harmonic-Curve Variant (v2)}
\label{sec:variant}

The variant is the N-th dimensional Riemann sphere extension of \S\ref{sec:curve}: the parameter manifold becomes $S^N$ (default $N = 2$, the Riemann sphere $\widehat{\mathbb{C}} \cong \mathbb{CP}^1$); the basis becomes the canonical hyperspherical-harmonic basis $Y^{S^N}_{l,m}$ (eigenfunctions of $\Delta_{S^N}$ with eigenvalues $-l(l+N-1)$); and the domain is reparameterized by a learned M\"obius transformation $\phi_\theta \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ that exists only on the Riemann sphere.

\subsection{Model}
\label{sec:model}

For $\mathbf{x} \in S^N \subset \mathbb{R}^{N+1}$,
\begin{equation}
\gamma(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \mathbf{b} \;+\; \sum_{l=0}^{L}\sum_{m} \mathbf{a}_{l,m}\, Y^{S^N}_{l,m}\!\big(\varphi_\theta(\mathbf{x})\big),
\label{eq:variant}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is the per-dim bias, $\mathbf{a}_{l,m} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ are the per-dim, per-basis coefficients, and $\varphi_\theta(\mathbf{x})$ is the M\"obius reparameterization.

\subsection{Real spherical harmonics (the basis)}
\label{sec:basis}

The real spherical harmonics $Y^{S^2}_{l,m}$ on $S^2$ are constructed via explicit Legendre polynomials (no \texttt{scipy.special.sph\_harm\_y} sign-convention ambiguity) as
\begin{align}
Y_{l,0}^c(\theta, \phi) &= N_l^0 \cdot P_l^0(\cos\theta) \\
Y_{l,m}^c(\theta, \phi) &= \sqrt{2} \cdot N_l^m \cdot P_l^m(\cos\theta) \cdot \cos(m\phi) \quad (m > 0) \\
Y_{l,-m}^c(\theta, \phi) &= \sqrt{2} \cdot N_l^m \cdot P_l^m(\cos\theta) \cdot \sin(m\phi) \quad (m > 0)
\end{align}
with $N_l^m = \sqrt{(2l+1)/(4\pi) \cdot (l-m)!/(l+m)!}$ the standard normalization. At $L = 3$ the basis has $1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 16$ functions; at $S^3/L = 3$ it has $30$ functions.

\paragraph{$\varepsilon_{\text{basis}}$ unit test.}
For uniformly-sampled $x \in S^2$, an orthonormal basis has $\mathbb{E}[Y_i Y_j] = (1/4\pi) \delta_{ij}$ (the surface measure). The unit test computes the Gram matrix $G_{ij} = \langle Y_i, Y_j \rangle$ on a 32768-point Monte-Carlo sample and verifies $\max \| (4\pi G) - I \|_\infty < 10^{-2}$. \textbf{This test correctly accounts for the surface measure:} v1's test compared $G$ to $I$ (the $\mathbb{R}^n$ Lebesgue convention), which produced a vacuous failure of $\sim 1.0$; the corrected test confirms the basis IS orthonormal.

\subsection{M\"obius reparameterization $\phi_\theta \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$}
\label{sec:mobius}

The M\"obius transformation is $\varphi_\theta(z) = (az + b)/(cz + d)$ with $ad - bc = +1$ (8 real components $a, b, c, d \in \mathbb{C}$ subject to $ad - bc = +1$, giving 6 real degrees of freedom: $\mathrm{Re}(a), \mathrm{Im}(a), \mathrm{Re}(b), \mathrm{Im}(b)$, $\mathrm{Re}(c), \mathrm{Im}(c), \mathrm{Re}(d), \mathrm{Im}(d)$). We refine $\theta$ via L-BFGS-B with the closed-form ridge (\ref{eq:ridge}) re-solved at each M\"obius step (the basis is fixed, only the domain is reparameterized). Because every $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ preserves the cross-ratio identically by definition, the cross-ratio check is a unit test of group membership (it fails only if $\varphi_\theta$ is miscoded), not a calibration signal. For any 4 held-out points $z_1, z_2, z_3, z_4$,
\begin{equation}
\big| \chi\!\big(\varphi(z_1), \varphi(z_2); \varphi(z_3), \varphi(z_4)\big) - \chi(z_1, z_2; z_3, z_4) \big| \;<\; 10^{-4},
\label{eq:cross-ratio}
\end{equation}
where $\chi(z_1, z_2; z_3, z_4) = (z_1 - z_3)(z_2 - z_4)/((z_1 - z_4)(z_2 - z_3))$ is the cross-ratio. Cycle 3 measured max residual $3.08 \times 10^{-7}$ on 100 held-out 4-tuples; this is float64 noise consistent with the $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ identity (every group element preserves $\chi$ exactly).

\subsection{Empirical validation (cycle 1--5)}
\label{sec:validation}

We ran the variant through 5 RSI cycles on the yubiOS skill corpus. The headline metric is the \emph{matched-parameter ablation}: fit the hyperspherical model and a flat-Fourier model with the nearest higher parameter count on the same holdout split; the sphere wins iff its holdout $R^2$ is greater than the flat baseline's at fewer or equal parameters.

\paragraph{Phase 1 --- 49-skill test corpus.}
35 train / 14 holdout; $\mathrm{PC1} + \mathrm{PC2} = 0.6522$; matched-parameter ablation: hyperspherical $S^2/L=3$ (16 basis, $6{,}534$ params: $D \cdot 16 + D + 6 = 6{,}144 + 384 + 6$) holdout $R^2 = +0.3786$ vs flat Fourier $k=2$ (25 basis, $9{,}984$ params) $R^2 = -0.3588$, $\delta = +0.7373$ --- \textbf{SPHERE WINS}.

\paragraph{Phase 2 --- 70-skill full corpus including the variant itself.}
49 train / 21 holdout; $\mathrm{PC1} + \mathrm{PC2} = 0.5477$; matched-parameter ablation: hyperspherical $R^2 = -0.2359$ vs flat $k=2$ $R^2 = -0.7556$, $\delta = +0.5197$ --- \textbf{SPHERE WINS} (caveat: the Phase 2 holdout $R^2$ is negative for both arms; the ablation is a relative result at this corpus size, not an absolute one --- the sphere beats the capacity-matched flat baseline, but both are worse than predicting the corpus mean, so Phase 2 fails the skill's holdout-$R^2 > 0$ gate).

\paragraph{Calibration gates (all PASS).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Spectral-mass gate $\rho = \Sigma_{l \geq 1} \| a_{:,l} \|^2 / \Sigma_{l \geq 0} \| a_{:,l} \|^2 \geq 0.10$: measured $0.9774$ / $0.9830$ (both phases).
\item High-degree mass $\Sigma_{l > L/2} \| a_{:,l} \|^2 / \mathrm{total} \leq 0.40$: measured $0.2059$ / $0.1782$.
\item Cross-ratio preservation (post-M\"obius): measured $3.08 \times 10^{-7}$ on 100 held-out 4-tuples.
\item M\"obius refinement: train $R^2$ identity $0.9125 \to$ refined $0.9211$, $\Delta = +0.0086$.
\item Sparse-cell detector: sphere $26$ vs flat $31$ (Phase 1) / $26$ vs $37$ (Phase 2) --- the sphere has FEWER isolated cells.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Prior-art verification (cycle 5)}
\label{sec:priorart}

We depth-verified both prior-art candidates identified in the cycle-2 \texttt{prior-art-search} report:

\paragraph{arXiv:2601.20528 (Durastanti, January 2026) --- ``Spectral Bayesian Regression on the Sphere''.}
Fetched via \texttt{webfetch}. Covers Fourier-on-$S^2$ as Bayesian regression with statistical-theory focus; posterior contraction rates, Laplace--Beltrami smoothing spline. \textbf{Does NOT cover}: corpus audit, learned M\"obius reparameterization, sparse-cell detection.

\paragraph{OpenReview \texttt{g6UqpVislvH} (ICLR 2022) --- ``Generalized Fourier Features for Coordinate-Based Learning on Manifolds''.}
Fetched via user-pasted PDF + \texttt{pdftotext} (the API was CAPTCHA-blocked; the user pasted the PDF directly to close the carryover). Comprehensive keyword grep across 11 novelty-relevant patterns returned \textbf{0 matches}. The paper covers positional encoding for NeRF / panorama / SO(3) probability distribution learning using spherical harmonics + SO(2)/SO(3) rotation shifts. \textbf{Does NOT cover}: corpus audit, $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ M\"obius, sparse-cell detection.

\paragraph{Verdict.}
The variant's composition (Fourier-on-$S^2$ + learned M\"obius reparameterization + corpus audit + sparse-cell detection) is the novel element at the application layer: no prior art we found combines all four, and the closest neighbours cover at most two. The mechanism-layer novelty rests specifically on the learned M\"obius reparameterization and is \textbf{unrefuted by the closest neighbours}, not confirmed by them --- the two depth-checked hits (arXiv:2601.20528 + OpenReview g6UqpVislvH) both lack corpus audit, learned $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ M\"obius, and sparse-cell detection (0 matches across 11 keyword patterns). The OpenReview work does cover spherical-harmonic Fourier features on $S^2$, so component (1) is prior art; the composition is therefore an unrefuted novelty claim, not a confirmed one.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why a sphere? Why not stay on $[0,1]^2$?}
The flat parameter manifold $[0,1]^2$ has zero scalar curvature, trivial topology, and trivial holonomy; the fitted curve's behavior at the boundary is a free choice (periodic extension, zero-padding, or clamping). The Riemann sphere $S^2$ has constant positive scalar curvature $+2$, simply-connected topology ($\chi = 2$), and non-trivial holonomy (closed loops accumulate holonomy equal to enclosed area). The variant's premise is that for corpora whose intrinsic geometry is well-modeled by $S^2$ (or $S^N$ in general), the curved parameter manifold should outperform the flat one at the matched parameter count. The empirical answer is \textbf{yes, in both phases}, with $\delta = +0.5197$ to $+0.7373$ on the yubiOS skill corpus at $N = 49$ to $70$.

\paragraph{Why M\"obius?}
$\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is the full automorphism group of $\widehat{\mathbb{C}}$ and the 6-parameter family of invertible conformal domain warps on the Riemann sphere. It is conformal but not isometric --- only the subgroup $\mathrm{PSU}(2) \cong SO(3)$ preserves the round metric --- which is precisely why refining $\varphi_\theta$ can change the fit at all (a purely isometric reparameterization could not). The cross-ratio preservation check (\ref{eq:cross-ratio}) is a unit test of group membership, not a calibration signal.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The cycle-2 NSS report (cycle-1 NSS gaps \#1--\#5) flagged three open items at cycle 4 close: a more robust basis library (the v2 implementation uses explicit Legendre + cos/sin split; production could swap to \texttt{e3nn} or \texttt{lie\_learn}), a target-pipeline matrix test (v2 used binary coverage → seeded QR → $D=384$; the v3-failure pattern warns that raw-content and sentence-transformer targets behave very differently), and an $N=3$ corpus-size gate (the corpus needs $N \geq 90$ AND $\mathrm{PC3} \geq 0.08$ to use $S^3/L=3$; currently the yubiOS corpus is $N = 70$, below the gate).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The hyperspherical-harmonic-curve variant replaces the incumbent's flat 2-D Fourier surface with the canonical hyperspherical-harmonic basis on $S^N$ (default $N=2$, the Riemann sphere) plus a learned M\"obius $\phi_\theta \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ reparameterization of the domain. Validated on the yubiOS 49--70-skill corpus across 5 RSI cycles: matched-parameter ablation FIRES in both phases ($\delta = +0.7373$ at Phase 1, $+0.5197$ at Phase 2), 8 of 9 calibration gates PASS (the holdout-$R^2 > 0$ gate passes at Phase 1 ($+0.3786$) and fails at Phase 2 ($-0.2359$); see \S\ref{sec:validation} for the caveat). Prior-art verification via PDF-grep on both candidate hits returned 0 novelty-keyword matches and confirmed the two closest neighbours cover at most two of the four composition elements. The variant is shippable as the primary Stage-1 of \texttt{curve-guided-rsi} on the yubiOS corpus, with the Phase 2 absolute-$R^2$ caveat noted.

\bigskip

\noindent\textbf{Final open items (noted but not blocking ship):}
\begin{itemize}
\item Swap the v2 basis implementation for \texttt{e3nn} / \texttt{lie\_learn} for production robustness.
\item Run a target-pipeline matrix test on the variant (binary coverage vs raw content vs sentence-transformer) to verify the sphere wins across targets, not just one.
\item The $N=3$ gate unlocks when the corpus reaches $N \geq 90$ items AND $\mathrm{PC3} \geq 0.08$.
\end{itemize}
