\title{Learned Latent Curves and the Hyperspherical-Harmonic Variant:\\
  {\large A Closed-Loop Framework for Corpus Embedding and Recursive Skill Auditing}}

\author{Shant Tchatalbachian}

\date{August 6, 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We describe a closed-loop framework for auditing and prioritizing improvement work across a corpus of engineering artifacts, and we document a sphere-aware extension of its curve-fitting stage together with the \emph{smallest audit unit} that the framework admits. The framework has four composed techniques: (1) a \emph{learned latent curve}, which re-expands a single 1-D ordering coordinate $t$ into a fixed-width $D$-dimensional embedding via a Fourier basis with learned frequencies; (2) \emph{curve-guided recursive self-improvement}, which uses sparse cells of the fitted curve as a prioritization lens for gap-mapping and bounded fixpoint-style editing cycles; (3) a \emph{hyperspherical-harmonic curve}, which replaces the flat $[0,1]^2$ parameter manifold of (1) with the Riemann sphere $S^2$ and a learned M\"obius $\phi_\theta \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ reparameterization; and (4) the \emph{single-action atom}, which is the smallest unit of the loop --- one corpus item maps to one point on $S^2$, one missing-primitive flip is one action, and the geodesic-only criterion gives an invariant that composes linearly across files. We give the governing equations for each technique, the empirical validation on a corpus of software skills ($N = 49$, $N = 70$, and $N = 79$ across three dated snapshots and one full-corpus audit), and a $474$-dispatch atom experiment on the $79$-skill corpus showing zero negative $\Delta$ and confirming Lemma~1 and Theorem~1 directly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background: The Learned-Latent-Curve Family}
\label{sec:family}

\subsection{Flat curve model}

For output dimension $j = 1, \dots, D$ (canonically $D = 384$) and 1-D coordinate $t \in [0, 1]$, the model is
\begin{equation}
z_j(t) = a_{j,0} + \sum_{m=1}^{k} \big( a_{j,m} \sin(2\pi f_m t) + b_{j,m} \cos(2\pi f_m t) \big),
\label{eq:flat}
\end{equation}
with $k$ shared learned frequencies $f_1, \dots, f_k$ and per-output coefficients $a_{j,m}, b_{j,m}$. Stacked over $j$, this is a curve $\gamma : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^D$. Writing the design vector
\begin{equation}
\phi(t) = \big[\, 1,\; \sin(2\pi f_1 t),\, \cos(2\pi f_1 t),\, \dots,\, \sin(2\pi f_k t),\, \cos(2\pi f_k t) \,\big] \in \mathbb{R}^{1+2k},
\label{eq:design}
\end{equation}
the model is $\gamma(t) = C\, \phi(t)$ with coefficient matrix $C \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times (1+2k)}$, giving a parameter count of
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{flat}} = D \cdot (1 + 2k).
\label{eq:paramflat}
\end{equation}
At $D = 384$, $k = 8$: $P_{\mathrm{flat}} = 384 \cdot 17 = 6{,}528$ parameters.

\subsection{Closed-form coefficient solve}

With the frequencies $f$ held fixed, the linear coefficient solve is the Tikhonov ridge,
\begin{equation}
C^{\star} = (\Phi^{\top}\Phi + \lambda I)^{-1} \Phi^{\top} Z,
\label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times (1+2k)}$ stacks the design vector and $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$ stacks the target vectors. This is the sanity floor for any gradient-descent fit at the same frequencies: a fit worse than this at the same $f$ means the optimizer failed, not the model.

\subsection{Obtaining the coordinate $t$}
The default pipeline sets $t$ from the top principal component of a $z$-scored $N \times D_{\mathrm{feat}}$ feature matrix, mapped to $[0,1]$; a rank-uniformized variant replaces raw PC1 scores by their fractional ranks before mapping. The PC1+PC2 explained-variance ratio is the fit-quality gate ($\geq 0.40$); flat curves below the gate are not used.

\section{The Hyperspherical-Harmonic Curve}
\label{sec:variant}

\subsection{Model}

For $\mathbf{x} \in S^2 \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ (the Riemann sphere),
\begin{equation}
\gamma(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{b} + \sum_{\ell=0}^{L} \sum_{m} \mathbf{a}_{\ell,m}\, Y^{S^2}_{\ell,m}\!\big(\phi_\theta(\mathbf{x})\big),
\label{eq:hyperspherical}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is the per-dim bias, $\mathbf{a}_{\ell,m} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ are the per-dim per-basis coefficients, and $\phi_\theta$ is the M\"obius reparameterization.

The parameter count is $D \cdot n_{\mathrm{basis}} + D + n_{\mathrm{M\"obius}}$, where $n_{\mathrm{basis}} = \sum_{\ell=0}^{L}(2\ell+1)$ and $n_{\mathrm{M\"obius}}$ is the real dimension of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ (Section~\ref{sec:mobius}). At $L=3$ on $S^2$, $n_{\mathrm{basis}}=16$; at $D=384$: $P_{\mathrm{sphere}} = 384 \cdot 16 + 384 + 6 = 6{,}534$.

\subsection{M\"obius reparameterization}
\label{sec:mobius}

$\phi_\theta(z) = (az+b)/(cz+d)$ with $a,b,c,d \in \mathbb{C}$ and $ad-bc=+1$. The four complex coefficients $a,b,c,d$ carry $8$ real components. The constraint $ad-bc=+1$ is a \emph{complex} constraint (real part equals 1, imaginary part equals 0), so it removes $2$ real degrees of freedom, leaving $6$ real degrees of freedom. The $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ identification (matrices $M$ and $-M$ map to the same M\"obius transformation) is a discrete identification and does not further reduce the real dimension. So $\phi_\theta$ has $6$ real degrees of freedom, parameterised by the real and imaginary parts of $a,b,c,d$ subject to $ad-bc=1$.

We refine $\theta$ via L-BFGS-B with the closed-form ridge (Eq.~\ref{eq:ridge}) re-solved at each step. The basis is fixed; only the domain is reparameterized. Because every $\varphi \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ preserves the cross-ratio identically by definition, the cross-ratio check verifies implementation consistency: it fails if $\varphi_\theta$ is miscoded, not if the learned map is a poor fit.

\subsection{Operational instantiation}
\label{sec:operational}

All material in this subsection was added on 2026-08-07 in PR~\#200; the title-page date 2026-08-06 reflects the body of the paper as submitted.

\begin{remark}[Fibonacci sampling and $Y_3^3$ angular probe --- operationalized as \texttt{rsi-phi-skill}, 2026-08-07]
The hyperspherical-harmonic variant of Eq.~\ref{eq:hyperspherical} is operationalized today (2026-08-07) as the \texttt{rsi-phi-skill} agent skill, which sits in the corpus as the bounded-recursive-self-improvement loop on the Fibonacci-sphere parameter manifold. The two design moves that operationalize the variant are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fibonacci index $i$ plays the role of $t$.} Sampling on $S^2$ is done with Vogel's golden-angle Fibonacci scheme,
\[
z_i \;=\; 1 - \frac{2i+1}{N}, \qquad
\phi_i \;=\; 2\pi\,\frac{i}{\varphi}, \qquad
\theta_i \;=\; \arccos(z_i), \qquad
\varphi \;=\; \frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},
\]
so that the corpus item at index $i$ IS the parameter point on the sphere --- no lookup table, $O(1)$ per point. This eliminates pole clustering at the diagnostic-grid stage (the standard latitude-longitude grid clusters at $\theta \to 0,\pi$; Fibonacci does not).
\item \textbf{Native basis $Y_3^3$ as the angular probe.} The 3-fold azimuthal spherical harmonic is,
\[
\Re\{Y_3^3(\theta,\phi)\} \;=\; K\,\sin^3\theta\,\cos(3\phi), \qquad
K \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{245}{64\pi}}
\]
(Condon--Shortley normalization, $K = \sqrt{(35\cdot 7)/(64\pi)}$). The $\sin^3\theta$ factor vanishes at the poles and peaks near the equator; the $\cos(3\phi)$ factor folds the 3-fold rotational symmetry into the embedding.
\end{enumerate}
For 384 symmetric azimuthal lobes, the per-item basis vector is $b_i = (\sin^3\theta_i\,\cos(m_1\phi_i),\ldots,\sin^3\theta_i\,\cos(m_{384}\phi_i))$ with $m_k = 3k$ for $k = 1,\ldots,128$ ($384 = 2^7 \cdot 3$). The skill \texttt{rsi-phi-skill} tests BOTH orderings $(\ell{=}128,\,m{=}256)$ and $(\ell{=}256,\,m{=}128)$ per cycle and picks the higher $\mathrm{PC1}{+}\mathrm{PC2}$ --- the gate that drives the regime. Today's data: $384$-D passes the $\mathrm{PC1}{+}\mathrm{PC2} \ge 0.40$ gate on the chosen variant $(\ell{=}384,\,m{=}3,\,\sin^{384}\theta\,\text{polar})$.
\end{remark}

The Fibonacci-sphere sampling and $Y_3^3$ angular-probe primitive above are the operational basis of the \texttt{rsi-phi-skill} agent skill (added 2026-08-07 to both \texttt{yubi-OS/yubiOS} and \texttt{yubi-OS/agent-skills}). The companion artifacts are:

\section{The Single-Action Atom and Linear Composition}
\label{sec:atom}

The smallest audit unit of the curve-guided framework is the \emph{single-action atom}. We define it precisely so the only-positive-$\Delta$ invariant it carries propagates linearly across the corpus.

\subsection{Atom: from file to $S^2$ point}

For a single corpus item $f$ (a file with $N_{\mathrm{sec}} \geq 2$ sections), the atom computes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \textbf{per-section coverage vector} $c_i \in \{0,1\}^9$, scored by pattern-matching against a fixed $9$-D binary primitive basis $\mathcal{P} = (p_0, \ldots, p_8)$.
\item A \textbf{weighted aggregate} $c = \sum_i w_i c_i$, with weights $w_i = \mathrm{len}(\mathrm{section}_i) / \mathrm{len}(f)$, thresholded at $0.5$.
\item A \textbf{section coverage matrix} $M \in \{0,1\}^{N_{\mathrm{sec}} \times 9}$, PCA top-$2$ via SVD giving $(\bar u, \bar v)$ for the file aggregate.
\item A \textbf{stereographic lift} $\sigma: \mathbb{R}^2 \to S^2$ with
\begin{equation}
\sigma(u,v) = \frac{1}{1+u^2+v^2}\big(2u,\; 2v,\; u^2+v^2-1\big) \in S^2,$
\label{eq:stereographic}
\end{equation}
giving one point $p = \sigma(\bar u, \bar v) \in S^2$ per file.
\item An \textbf{ideal pole} $p^* = \sigma(\bar u^*, \bar v^*)$, where $(\bar u^*, \bar v^*)$ is the lift of the all-ones coverage vector $(1,\dots,1)$.
\item A \textbf{geodesic gap} $d(f) = \|p - p^*\|_2$ (chordal proxy on $S^2$).
\end{enumerate}
For each missing primitive $i \in \{j : c_j = 0\}$, the atom simulates the flip $c_i := 1$, recomputes the $S^2$ point $p'$, and selects $i^* = \arg\min_i d_{\mathrm{post}}(f)$. This is the \emph{geodesic-only criterion}: the chosen action strictly minimizes post-flip geodesic distance to the ideal pole.

\subsection{Lemma 1 (atom invariant)}

\begin{lemma}
For any file $f$ and any action $\alpha$ selected by the geodesic-only criterion, $\Delta_f = d_{\mathrm{pre}} - d_{\mathrm{post}} \geq 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The criterion selects $\alpha^* = \arg\min_i d_{\mathrm{post}}$ over the $k$ candidates where $k = |\{j : c_j = 0\}|$. Each candidate flips exactly one missing primitive $i$ from $0$ to $1$ and recomputes the file's $S^2$ point. If all $k$ candidates had $d_{\mathrm{post}} \geq d_{\mathrm{pre}}$, the $\arg\min$ would tie at $d_{\mathrm{pre}}$ and $\Delta_f = 0$, never negative. A strictly negative $\Delta_f$ would require $d_{\mathrm{post}} > d_{\mathrm{pre}}$ for the $\arg\min$, contradicting the minimization. The action space is append-only (single-primitive flips); no candidate removes coverage.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theorem 1 (linear composition)}

\begin{theorem}
For a corpus $C$ with $|C| = N$ files, every multi-file action $\alpha_{\mathrm{corpus}} = (\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_N)$ where each $\alpha_i$ is an atomic action on file $f_i$, has corpus-level
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{corpus}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \Delta_{f_i}.
\label{eq:composition}
\end{equation}
If every atomic $\Delta \geq 0$, then $\Delta_{\mathrm{corpus}} \geq 0$, and $\Delta_{\mathrm{corpus}} > 0$ if at least one atomic $\Delta > 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each $\alpha_i$ operates on its own file $f_i$ independently: the per-file coverage matrix and $S^2$ point are unchanged for all $f_j$ with $j \neq i$. The geodesic distance $d_{f_i}$ is a function of $f_i$'s coverage alone. Linear sum of non-negative scalars is non-negative.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Corollary 1 (cumulative monotonicity)}

\begin{corollary}[Cumulative monotonicity]
If every atomic $\Delta_{f_i} \geq 0$, the corpus-level cumulative $\Delta_{\mathrm{corpus}}$ is monotone non-decreasing as further cycles are appended: writing $\Delta^{(n)}_{\mathrm{corpus}} = \sum_{c=1}^{n} \sum_{i} \Delta^{(c)}_{f_i}$ for the running total after $n$ cycles, $\Delta^{(n+1)}_{\mathrm{corpus}} \geq \Delta^{(n)}_{\mathrm{corpus}}$ for every $n$. The per-cycle increment $\sum_i \Delta^{(c)}_{f_i}$ is not constrained in shape and need not decrease monotonically in $c$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each cycle contributes $\sum_i \Delta^{(c)}_{f_i} \geq 0$ by Lemma~1 and Eq.~\ref{eq:composition}; appending a non-negative term to a running sum cannot decrease it. No claim is made about the ordering of successive increments, since Lemma~1 bounds each $\Delta_{f_i}$ from below only.
\end{proof}

\subsection{M\"obius refinement strategy}

The corpus-level $\phi_\theta \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ is one M\"obius transformation applied uniformly to all files; the atom-bound composition rule (Theorem~1) requires it \emph{stable across per-cycle atom dispatches}, otherwise per-file $\Delta$s are measured in different charts and the linear sum does not apply to the reported cycles. Reported runs use the \emph{refine-once, $\phi_\theta$ frozen} mode ($\phi_\theta$ fit at corpus creation, frozen for all subsequent cycles); the joint-refine-per-cycle mode is reserved for $N_{\mathrm{items}} < 30$ or corpus growth $> 25\%$ since last refine.

\section{Dataset and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:dataset}

\subsection{Dataset}

The corpus consists of software-skill specifications (engineering artifacts) in the yubiOS repository. Each item has a 9-dimensional binary feature vector recording which of nine primitive capabilities the skill implements. The corpus has grown across three dated snapshots: the alphabetical-first-half (49 items, $N_{\mathrm{train}}=35$, $N_{\mathrm{holdout}}=14$), the full directory at the time of the headline ablation (70 items, $N_{\mathrm{train}}=49$, $N_{\mathrm{holdout}}=21$), and the complete skill directory at this paper's revision (79 items, dated 2026-08-06, used for the corpus-audit RSI in Section~\ref{sec:results-audit} and the 474-dispatch atom experiment in Section~\ref{sec:results-atom}).

The 9-D binary feature space has principal-component concentration $\mathrm{PC1}+\mathrm{PC2} = 0.652$ at the 49-item snapshot and $0.548$ at the 70-item snapshot; both clear the $\mathrm{PC1} \geq 0.40$ gate (Section~\ref{sec:family}).

\subsection{Baseline}

The capacity-matched baseline is a flat Fourier curve on $[0,1]^2$ with $k=2$ (the 2-D tensor-product extension of Eq.~\ref{eq:flat}), giving $5 \times 5 = 25$ basis functions and $9{,}984$ parameters. We choose $k=2$ because it is the lowest-order 2-D tensor product that exceeds the 16-function count of the spherical variant ($16$ basis, $6{,}534$ parameters); $k=1$ gives only $3 \times 3 = 9$ basis and would not be a capacity match for the 16-function sphere, while $k=3$ gives $7 \times 7 = 49$ basis and $\sim 24$k parameters, which overwhelms the corpus. Both models are fitted with the closed-form ridge (Eq.~\ref{eq:ridge}) and the same ridge regularisation $\lambda$.

\subsection{Evaluation metric}

The headline metric is \emph{matched-parameter ablation}: holdout $R^2$ at fewer or equal parameters, with the same split and the same ridge regularisation. We report the absolute holdout $R^2$ (not just the delta) so the result is interpretable against the corpus-mean baseline ($R^2 = 0$).

\subsection{Reproducibility}

All headline ablation numbers are single-run point estimates (no error bars) on a fixed holdout split, with a shared ridge regularisation $\lambda$ across both arms. The audit-phase numbers (Section~\ref{sec:results-audit}) carry 5-seed $\pm$ std error bars for phases E--H, where the 5-seed multi-cycle stress test was run; the headline ablation does not. All audit runs reported in this paper use the 79-skill corpus dated 2026-08-06 (single dated snapshot, referenced throughout).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Hyperspherical-harmonic variant: matched-parameter ablation}
\label{sec:results-ablation}

The matched-parameter ablation on the two headline corpus snapshots is the paper's central empirical claim: on these corpora, the hyperspherical parameter manifold is a strictly better inductive bias than the flat $[0,1]^2$ baseline, with the absolute holdout $R^2$ positive on the smaller snapshot and the relative $\delta$ positive on both snapshots.

\textbf{49-item snapshot ($N_{\mathrm{train}}=35$, $N_{\mathrm{holdout}}=14$):}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hyperspherical model} (S$^2$/$L{=}3$, 16 basis functions, 6,534 parameters): $R^2_{\mathrm{holdout}} = +0.618$.
\item \textbf{Flat Fourier baseline} ($k{=}2$ on $[0,1]^2$, 25 basis functions, 9,984 parameters): $R^2_{\mathrm{holdout}} = -0.359$.
\item \textbf{Matched-parameter $\delta$}: $+0.977$. The sphere wins by nearly 1.0 $R^2$ units at fewer parameters; the flat baseline is worse than the corpus-mean prediction.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{70-item snapshot ($N_{\mathrm{train}}=49$, $N_{\mathrm{holdout}}=21$, variant-included):}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hyperspherical model}: $R^2_{\mathrm{holdout}} = +0.222$.
\item \textbf{Flat Fourier baseline}: $R^2_{\mathrm{holdout}} = -1.120$.
\item \textbf{Matched-parameter $\delta$}: $+1.342$. The sphere wins by more than 1.3 $R^2$ units on a corpus where the flat baseline is strictly worse than predicting the corpus mean.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What this result does and does not show}

The hyperspherical-harmonic variant wins on the matched-parameter ablation at both corpus snapshots by a margin (49-item $\delta = +0.977$, 70-item $\delta = +1.342$) that is hard to attribute to noise. We do \emph{not} claim the variant is a strict improvement over the flat curve in absolute terms: on the 70-item snapshot the absolute $R^2$ is $+0.222$ (positive but small), and the headline numbers are single-run point estimates without error bars. The atom experiment (Figure~2) shows the smallest audit unit composes without regression --- 474 dispatches, 0 negative $\Delta$ --- but does not by itself validate the variant.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The matched-parameter ablation in Section~\ref{sec:results-ablation} reports single-seed point estimates of $\delta = +0.977$ (49-item) and $\delta = +1.342$ (70-item) on the yubiOS software-skill corpus. A second-corpus re-run of the ablation itself would replace these with error bars at the headline-ablation level and is the one remaining open item. Separately, the risk that the ablation measures fit rather than inductive bias is addressed by the synthetic-manifold benchmark of Appendix~\ref{app:20cycle}.3, which runs the same matched-capacity comparison against off-span targets on a known $T^2$ negative control and a known $S^2$ positive control; the prediction check and its statistics are reported there.


\section{Conclusion}

The hyperspherical-harmonic curve replaces the flat $[0,1]^2$ parameter manifold with the Riemann sphere $S^2$ and learns a M\"obius reparameterization $\phi_\theta \in \mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ of the domain. On the yubiOS software-skill corpus, it wins on the matched-parameter ablation at both dated snapshots (49-item $\delta = +0.977$, 70-item $\delta = +1.342$), with fewer parameters and no error bars. The single-action atom is the smallest unit of the resulting audit pipeline; its only-positive-$\Delta$ invariant propagates linearly to multi-file composition (Theorem~1) and to the cumulative total across cycles (Corollary~1), as confirmed by $1391$ atom dispatches across the three corpora (\texttt{skills/}, \texttt{docs/}, \texttt{refs/}) in Appendix~\ref{app:multi-corpus} and a $20$-cycle experiment on the $11$-file deep-research corpus in Appendix~\ref{app:20cycle}, showing zero negative $\Delta$ across all of them. The synthetic-manifold benchmark in Appendix~\ref{app:20cycle}.3 stress-tests the inductive-bias claim with off-span targets on both a negative and a positive control; the headline numbers are reported there. A second-corpus re-run of the ablation itself remains the one remaining open item and would replace the present single-seed point estimates with error bars.

\section{Multi-Corpus RSI Audit --- \texttt{skills/}, \texttt{docs/}, \texttt{refs/}}
\label{app:multi-corpus}

The $79$-skill audit reported in Section~\ref{sec:results-atom} used cycles 10--15 of the \texttt{curve-guided-rsi} self pipeline; cycles 1--9 were the prior phases A--H audit on the $70$-skill corpus. That audit has since been extended from the engineering corpus to the whole repository. This appendix reports the multi-corpus run: the same single-action-atom pipeline, the same 9-D \texttt{internal-big-picture} primitive basis, the same chordal proxy on $S^2$ with $\phi_\theta$ frozen at identity, applied independently to the self-documentation corpus (\texttt{docs/}, $21$ files) and the references corpus (\texttt{refs/}, $113$ files) alongside the engineering corpus (\texttt{skills/}, $79$ files). All three reach corpus-level fixpoint.

\subsection{Headline totals}

\textbf{$1391$ atom dispatches across $213$ files, $598$ strictly positive $\Delta$, zero negative $\Delta$, cumulative $\Delta = +59.7671$.} By corpus:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{skills/} --- $79$ files, $6$ cycles, $474$ dispatches, $116$ positive, $\Delta = +11.2963$, sparse cells $7 \to 0$.
\item \texttt{docs/} --- $21$ files, $6$ cycles, $126$ dispatches, $56$ positive, $\Delta = +5.5410$, sparse cells $7 \to 0$.
\item \texttt{refs/} --- $113$ files, $7$ cycles, $791$ dispatches, $426$ positive, $\Delta = +42.9298$, sparse cells $33 \to 0$.
\end{itemize}
All three corpora terminate on the same rule (peak $\Delta < 0.001$) and all three reach $100\%$ coverage on all nine primitives. This is the verification of Lemma~1 at scale: $1391$ independent geodesic arg-min selections, not one of which produced a regression. Source: \texttt{papers/data/rsi-3-corpus-summary-2026-08-06.json}.

\subsection{Differential curve baselines}

Raw cumulative $\Delta$ is not comparable across corpora of different size, so the differential baseline normalizes by corpus size: mean $\Delta$ per file per cycle. \texttt{skills/} opens at $0.0706$ $\Delta$/file and decays $0.0381, 0.0162, 0.0112, 0.0069, 0$. \texttt{docs/} opens at $0.0929$ and decays $0.0795, 0.0604, 0.0225, 0.0086, 0$. \texttt{refs/} opens at $0.0881$, \emph{rises} to $0.1061$ at cycle 2, then decays $0.0918, 0.0657, 0.0259, 0.0024, 0$. The \texttt{refs/} corpus is the one departure from the monotone diminishing-returns shape seen elsewhere in this paper: per-cycle $\Delta$ on \texttt{refs/} goes $+9.9559 \to +11.9840 \to +10.3710 \to +7.4256 \to +2.9221 \to +0.2712 \to 0$, peaking at cycle~2. Corollary~1 constrains the \emph{cumulative} sum to be monotone non-decreasing, which it is; it says nothing about the per-cycle increment. The mechanism is the $k$-quantization of Section~A.3: \texttt{refs/} begins with most files deep in the high-$k$ tail ($k = 7$--$9$, where $\Delta$ is small), cycle~1 walks them up into the $k \asymp 5$ band where $\Delta$ is maximal, and cycle~2 therefore harvests more total distance than cycle~1 did.

\subsection{What transfers across corpora}

Three properties hold identically on all three corpora and are therefore properties of the atom rather than of the engineering corpus. \textbf{First}, the $\Delta$ ladder: the realized $\Delta$ depends only on $k$, the count of missing primitives, with the same nine values and the same unimodal peak at $k = 5$, on \texttt{skills/}, \texttt{docs/} and \texttt{refs/} alike. \textbf{Second}, the peak-$\Delta$ termination sequence: all three corpora walk down the same ladder ($0.1206 \to \ldots \to 0.0904 \to 0$) as the highest-$\Delta$ band is exhausted. \textbf{Third}, the identity of the laggard primitive: \texttt{cryptographic\_identity} is the last column to saturate in every corpus. The geodesic-only criterion has the least incentive to act on a primitive missing from files already close to the ideal pole.

\subsection{Scope of the multi-corpus claim}

The multi-corpus audit strengthens the atom-invariant claim (Lemma~1 and Theorem~1 now hold over $1391$ dispatches on three structurally distinct corpora rather than $474$ on one) but it does \emph{not} strengthen the headline matched-parameter ablation of Section~\ref{sec:results-ablation}, which remains a single-seed point estimate on one corpus family. The second-corpus re-run called for in Section~\ref{sec:limitations} would have to re-run the \emph{ablation}, not the audit, and has not been done.

\section{The 20-Cycle Deep-Research Corpus and the Synthetic-Manifold Benchmark}
\label{app:20cycle}

Section~\ref{sec:results} and Appendix~\ref{app:multi-corpus} report corpus-scale audits in which each file receives at most one dispatch per cycle. A complementary experiment runs the atom repeatedly against a small corpus to expose the per-file convergence behaviour that a wide, shallow audit averages away. This appendix reports that experiment and then reports the synthetic-manifold benchmark that stress-tests the inductive-bias claim.

\subsection{$20$ cycles on $11$ deep-research files}

The $11$-file deep-research corpus (the \texttt{sealed-uki-vm} and \texttt{falco} outputs in the yubiOS references tree) was audited over $20$ atom cycles --- an initial sweep plus seven post-edit re-fits --- with the corpus re-fitted after every applied edit, so each cycle sees a genuinely changed manifold. Zero cycles produced a negative $\Delta$. Cumulative $\Delta$ plateaued at $+1.6882$. Source: \texttt{papers/data/single-action-curve-rsi-cycles-2026-08-05.json}.

\subsection{Diminishing marginal value and the shifting peak}

The three peak runs trace $+0.3092$ (cycle~2, advisor-report, target \texttt{has\_source}) $\to +0.2705$ (the same file re-selected after its first edit, with a smaller $\Delta$ because the edit moved it closer to the ideal pole) $\to +0.1872$ (cycle~14, falco). Peak $\Delta$ fell $39.5\%$ across the three peak runs and mean $\Delta$ fell $28.2\%$, from $+0.1176$ to $+0.0844$. Per-file $\Delta$ reductions after editing: advisor-report $-55.7\%$, pkcs11-ecdsa-deepdive $-66.6\%$, pkcs11-ecdsa-VERIFIED $-47.5\%$, prior-art-V52 $-43.4\%$, comparative-report $-100\%$. Four of the eleven files reached a local minimum ($\Delta = 0$) and required no further action, up from two at cycle~12. The atom sweeps the corpus rather than over-fitting one file --- the same mechanism that produces the cycle-2 rise on \texttt{refs/} in Section~B.2.

\subsection{Synthetic-manifold benchmark (executed)}

Every empirical result in this paper is measured on corpora that were \emph{assumed} to suit an $S^2$ parameter manifold. That makes the matched-parameter ablation a test of fit, not of inductive bias. The benchmark that would separate the two is a negative control. \textbf{Corpus:} $N = 200$ synthetic points per manifold, sampled directly from the manifold (true manifold coordinates; no 9-bit feature encoding). \textbf{Manifolds:} $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$ (torus, genus~1) as negative control; $S^2$ (unit sphere) as positive control. \textbf{Arms:} the hyperspherical-harmonic curve on $S^2$ ($L = 3$, 16 real spherical-harmonic basis functions) against a flat \emph{periodic} 2-D Fourier basis on the raw manifold angles (16 functions, tensor product of $\{1, \sin\theta, \cos\theta, \sin 2\theta\}$ by $\{1, \sin\phi, \cos\phi, \cos 2\phi\}$ on the torus; longitude/latitude on the sphere). \textbf{Targets:} the $T^2$ target is the 3-term smooth function $\sin\theta + 0.5\cos\phi + 0.3\sin(\theta+\phi)$ (in the flat periodic Fourier $K{=}2$ span; the sphere arm's stereographic lift from $(\theta/(2\pi) - 0.5, \phi/(2\pi) - 0.5)$ is discontinuous at $\theta = 2\pi$, so the sphere arm cannot fit it perfectly). The v4 $S^2$ target is the degree-3 spherical harmonic $Y_3^3$ class, $\cos^3(\mathrm{lat}) \cdot \cos(3 \cdot \mathrm{lon})$ in (lon, lat) coordinates, plus additive Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.01^2)$ to test generalization rather than exact interpolation. The new $S^2$ target is in the SH $L{=}3$ span (it IS the Y_3^3 basis function) but \emph{not} in the flat periodic Fourier $K{=}2$ span on (lon, lat): $\cos(3 \cdot \mathrm{lon})$ is not in $\mathrm{span}\{1, \sin\mathrm{lon}, \cos\mathrm{lon}, \sin 2\mathrm{lon}\}$ (Chebyshev's identity requires $\cos(2 \cdot \mathrm{lon})$ which is absent from the basis), and $\cos^3(\mathrm{lat})$ is not in $\mathrm{span}\{1, \sin\mathrm{lat}, \cos\mathrm{lat}, \cos 2\mathrm{lat}\}$ (it requires $\cos(3 \cdot \mathrm{lat})$). The v3 $S^2$ target $x + 0.5y + 0.3z$ was in the span of both bases up to floating-point precision, so the v3 sphere-wins result was a paired-t statistic on $\mathrm{np.linalg.solve}$ round-off, not inductive bias; the v4 off-span target makes the inductive-bias signal measurable at the numerical scale, not the floating-point scale. The v2 non-periodic flat basis $\cos(i\pi u)\cos(j\pi v)$ on $[0,1]^2$ is dropped because it does not wrap at $u=1$ and was the basis artifact the v3 revision fixes. Both arms have the same number of basis functions (16), so capacity is matched.

\textbf{v3 protocol:} per-arm $\lambda$ sweep ($\mathrm{logspace}(-6, 1, 29)$, 29 candidates) on a train-only inner split (75/25 of the train rows, 60/20/20 of $N$ overall), refit on the full train rows with the selected $\lambda$, evaluate on the outer 20\% holdout. Same protocol for both arms; what differs is the basis, not the regularization. \textbf{Lift:} manifold-aware ground-truth coordinates --- $T^2$ tests feed $(\theta, \phi) \in [0, 2\pi)^2$ directly to both arms (sphere lifts via stereographic from the south pole after centring, flat feeds raw angles into the periodic basis); $S^2$ tests feed $(x,y,z)$ directly to the sphere arm and $(\mathrm{lon}, \mathrm{lat}) = (\mathrm{atan2}(y,x), \mathrm{arcsin}(z))$ to the flat arm. No PCA, no min-max, no 9-bit encoding.

\textbf{50 seeds}, each seed: fresh RNG $\to$ fresh data $\to$ fresh 80/20 outer split $\to$ fresh inner split $\to$ fresh per-arm $\lambda$ selection. \textbf{Prediction:} the flat baseline wins on $T^2$ (a sphere is the wrong prior for a genus-1 manifold) while the sphere wins on an $S^2$-generated positive control. Significance threshold: paired $t$-test on per-seed $\Delta = \mathrm{flat} - \mathrm{sphere}$ with $p < 0.05$.

\textbf{Prediction check (paired $p < 0.05$):} \emph{both predictions of the inductive-bias claim hold under the v4 off-span protocol.} On $T^2$ the periodic flat arm decisively wins ($+1.0000 \pm 0.0000$ vs sphere's $+0.9814 \pm 0.0110$, paired $p = 3.87 \times 10^{-16}$, flat wins $50/50$ seeds) --- the periodic 2-D Fourier basis on raw angles is the natural prior for a periodic target, while the stereographic lift to $S^2$ prevents the sphere arm from wrapping at $\theta = 2\pi$, so the 16 spherical-harmonic functions cannot fit the periodic target perfectly. On $S^2$ the sphere arm decisively wins ($+0.9995 \pm 0.0001$ vs flat's $-0.0825 \pm 0.0840$, paired $p = 2.47 \times 10^{-56}$, sphere wins $50/50$ seeds) --- the sphere arm fits the in-span $Y_3^3$-class target to the noise floor ($\sigma = 0.01$ noise caps $R^2$ at $\approx 0.9995$), while the flat arm's 16-function Fourier basis on (lon, lat) cannot represent $\cos^3(\mathrm{lat}) \cdot \cos(3 \cdot \mathrm{lon})$ above its 16-D truncation subspace (residual mean $R^2 \approx -0.08$). The inductive-bias signal is now measurable at the inductive-bias scale, not the floating-point scale. The synthetic $T^2$ target is the 3-term smooth function $\sin\theta + 0.5\cos\phi + 0.3\sin(\theta+\phi)$, well within the capacity of either basis; a noisier or higher-mode $T^2$ target is the natural next step (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The v3 reported sphere-wins-$S^2$ at 50 seeds with paired $p = 0.0002$ was a paired-t statistic on $\mathrm{np.linalg.solve}$ round-off residue (both arms reached $R^2 = 1.0000$ on the in-span target); v4 fixes this by moving the $S^2$ target out of the flat arm's span. The v1 reported flat-on-$T^2$ at 5 seeds was an artifact of (i) PCA-on-9-bits train/holdout leakage, (ii) a fixed $\lambda = 10^{-3}$ that mis-conditioned the sphere Gram matrix, and (iii) one outlier flat-arm seed at $R^2 = -7.35$ on the $S^2$ test; all three are closed in v2/v3.

\textbf{Implication:} the synthetic-manifold benchmark demonstrates that the inductive-bias claim holds \emph{when the targets are out-of-span for both bases}. The hyperspherical-harmonic variant wins on real $S^2$-structured corpora \emph{because} $S^2$ is the right manifold for that data, not because of capacity alone --- capacity is matched at 16 functions per arm, and the v4 $S^2$ target is chosen so that the flat arm's 16-function basis genuinely cannot fit it (residual $R^2 \approx -0.08$, not a floating-point artifact). The flat periodic basis wins on $T^2$ by the same mechanism: when the data is genuinely periodic, a periodic basis is the right prior. The benchmark discriminates: matching the basis to the manifold matters, and the right basis is not always the sphere. The remaining open item is a second-corpus re-run of the headline ablation itself (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Manifold-Coordinate Benchmark (Rigorous Re-Test)}
\label{app:manifold-coord}

This appendix complements the primary synthetic-manifold benchmark (Appendix~\ref{app:20cycle}.3, 50 seeds, off-span targets, capacity-matched at 16 basis functions per arm) with a second test that specifically probes the input-representation inductive bias on the smaller flat $K{=}2$ basis (rank 9 effective). Under the primary off-span protocol, that smaller flat basis loses $T^2$ to the sphere by capacity alone (16 SH functions $>$ 9 flat effective); the re-test reported here confirms both predictions of the inductive-bias claim under a \emph{partial-in-span} target design that gives the flat arm a realistic fitting advantage on the in-span component.

\subsection{Fix A targets (verified by lstsq at $N = 4000$)}

The Fix A design splits the in-span and out-of-span contributions to discriminate the topology signal from the capacity signal:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$T^2$ target: $\sin\theta\cos\phi + 0.5\sin(2\theta)\cos(2\phi)$.} The mode-1 component $\sin\theta\cos\phi$ IS in the smaller flat $K{=}2$ span (lstsq $R^2 = 1.0000$ on this component alone, fitted exactly by the periodic Fourier basis at $K{=}2$). The mode-2 component $\sin(2\theta)\cos(2\phi)$ is OUT of flat $K{=}2$ span (lstsq $R^2 = 0.0019$ on this component alone --- $K{=}2$ has no $\sin(2\theta)\cos(2\phi)$ basis function). On the COMBINED target (1:0.5 mode weighting): flat lstsq $R^2 = 0.8001$, sphere lstsq $R^2 = 0.5775$ --- flat wins $T^2$ at lstsq because the in-span component is dominant and the sphere arm's stereographic lift cannot wrap at $\theta = 2\pi$ (the stereo lift is discontinuous across the seam, so the sphere arm fits the periodic mode-1 component only partially).
\item \textbf{$S^2$ target: real $Y_3^3 = \sin^3(\mathrm{colatitude})\cos(3\phi)$}, where colatitude $\theta_c = \arccos(z)$ and azimuth $\phi = \mathrm{atan2}(y, x)$. This IS the $Y_3^3$ basis function (in SH $L{=}3$ span, lstsq $R^2 = 1.0000$ exactly). It is NOT in flat periodic Fourier $K{=}2$ span on (lon, lat): $\cos(3\phi)$ requires mode 3 which $K{=}2$ does not have, and $\sin^3(\mathrm{lat})$ is not in the $K{=}2$ tensor-product Fourier span (which contains only $\{1, \sin\mathrm{lat}, \cos\mathrm{lat}, \sin 2\mathrm{lat}, \cos 2\mathrm{lat}\}$ in the lat axis). On the COMBINED target: sphere lstsq $R^2 = 1.0000$, flat lstsq $R^2 = 0.0022$ --- sphere wins $S^2$ at lstsq.
\end{itemize}

The partial-in-span design discriminates: where one arm has a basis-fit advantage (the mode-1 component on $T^2$ for flat; the $Y_3^3$ basis function for sphere on $S^2$), the topology matters where BOTH arms must extrapolate (the mode-2 component is out-of-span for both arms on $T^2$). No noise on either target (clean basis-fit + topology-only comparison).

\subsection{Interpretation}

Both predictions of the inductive-bias claim hold under the Fix A partial-in-span design on the smaller flat $K{=}2$ basis. The honest read is that the claim \emph{survives the more rigorous test at both controls, at the smaller basis capacity}. On $T^2$, the negative control works because the in-span mode-1 component is dominant (lstsq floor 0.80) and the sphere arm's stereo lift cannot wrap at $\theta = 2\pi$. On $S^2$, the positive control works because the real $Y_3^3$ target is in the SH $L{=}3$ span (lstsq floor 1.0) and out of the flat $K{=}2$ span on (lon, lat) (lstsq floor 0.002).

\textbf{Capacity-confound context.} The flat basis has rank 9 effective (7 zero columns from $\sin(0\!\cdot\!\theta)=0$, $\sin(0\!\cdot\!\phi)=0$, and the four zero-multiplications in the tensor product). The sphere arm has rank 16 SH. Without the Fix A partial-in-span design --- for example, when the primary protocol's off-span targets are run against this smaller flat basis --- the sphere arm wins $T^2$ by capacity alone (16 SH $>$ 9 flat effective), as observed in the corresponding re-run. Fix A gives flat a real fitting advantage on the $T^2$ mode-1 component --- and even with that advantage, flat's $R^2 \approx 0.78$ reflects genuine partial-fit on the 1:0.5 mode weighting (the lstsq floor on the combined target is 0.80, not 1.0). The benchmark discriminates: the in-span mode-1 component is exactly fit (lstsq 1.0), the out-of-span mode-2 component is not (lstsq 0.002), and the measured $R^2 = 0.78$ is the in-span-fit share of the target's $R^2$-weighted signal.

\textbf{Limitations specific to manifold-coord parameterization.} (1) The $T^2$ target has a 0.5-weight mode-2 component that is genuinely out of span for both arms --- the lstsq floor on the combined target is 0.80, not 1.0. A pure mode-1 target would let flat reach 1.0 and would not test extrapolation; a pure mode-2 target would let neither arm reach a meaningful level. The 1:0.5 mode weighting is a deliberate balance. (2) The $S^2$ target is a single SH basis function ($Y_3^3$); higher-degree or non-smooth $S^2$ targets would be a more discriminating positive control. The primary benchmark adds $\sigma = 0.01$ Gaussian noise to the $S^2$ target; the re-test reported here omits noise (clean basis-fit + topology-only comparison). (3) Both targets are noiseless here; adding noise would test generalization rather than exact interpolation, but the lstsq verification is cleaner without noise. (4) The benchmark uses the smaller flat $K{=}2$ basis (rank 9 effective); the primary benchmark in Appendix~\ref{app:20cycle}.3 uses a flat basis at the same $K{=}2$ (rank 9 effective at $K{=}2$) but under the primary off-span protocol. Both protocols converge on the same conclusion --- the inductive-bias claim holds when the target is chosen out-of-span for both bases.

\textbf{Open-item status update} (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The primary protocol in Appendix~\ref{app:20cycle}.3 (50 seeds, off-span targets, capacity-matched at 16 basis functions per arm, $\sigma = 0.01$ noise on the $S^2$ target) is the primary test of the inductive-bias claim; this appendix is the second test at the smaller basis capacity under the Fix A partial-in-span design (no noise). Both controls work under both protocols; the remaining open items are a higher-degree $S^2$ positive control and a second-corpus re-run of the ablation itself.

\subsection{Per-dimension fit plots and the concentration gate}\label{app:use-cases:fits}
Table~\ref{tab:use-cases:gate} reports the PC1+PC2 concentration for each basis dimension against the $\ge 0.40$ gate, and Figure~\ref{fig:use-cases:fits} shows the corresponding per-dimension fit plots side by side. The five dimensions are not five independent experiments --- they are a nested sweep of the input representation on (nearly) the same corpus:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{7-D} --- the original 7-D binary primitive basis on the \texttt{refs/} corpus alone (1862 items). PC1+PC2 $= 1.0000$; this passes trivially, since 7-D $\subset$ 9-D and a single-corpus sample of this size concentrates perfectly. We report it as the floor case, not as evidence.
\item \textbf{9-D} --- the full 9-D binary primitive basis across all four corpora (2286 sections). PC1+PC2 $= 0.4565$: the paper's headline dimension, clearing the gate with real margin on a genuinely heterogeneous corpus.
\item \textbf{16-D} --- 16 real spherical harmonics on $S^2$ (SH $L{=}3$) over the same 2286 sections. PC1+PC2 $= 0.4627$, statistically indistinguishable from 9-D: moving from binary primitives to the harmonic basis at matched corpus neither helps nor hurts concentration.
\item \textbf{24-D} --- the extended feature space, 9-D $+$ 12 NSS (pod-security-standards) $+$ 3 meta, same 2286 sections. PC1+PC2 $= 0.2993$, \emph{failing} the gate. This is the informative negative: the concentration is a property of the primitive basis, and it does not survive arbitrary feature-space widening.
\item \textbf{384-D} --- the Fibonacci-sphere $Y_3^3$ variant ($\ell = 384$, $m = 3$) over 2610 items. PC1+PC2 $= 1.0000$ for the chosen variant, the strongest concentration in the sweep.
\end{itemize}
